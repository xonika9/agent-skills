# Harvesting loop learnings

Promote a lesson into durable guidance only after real runs provide evidence.

## Record before promoting

Record a significant one-off failure, recovery, or useful intervention when it may help resume the current run, compare later runs, or formulate a testable candidate lesson. A structured run journal is optional; use one when recovery or cross-run comparison changes a real decision, and keep it concise enough to remain maintainable.

## Promote when

- the same failure or successful intervention appears across more than one representative run;
- the lesson changes a decision, tool contract, stop condition, or validation method;
- the evidence is linked and the scope is clear;
- keeping the rule costs less than rediscovering the lesson.

## Record

- observed task/run identifiers;
- the failure mode and its consequence;
- the intervention;
- evidence that the intervention helped or did not;
- scope, counterexamples, and freshness;
- where the resulting rule belongs: global policy, repository context, domain skill, runtime adapter, or temporary operational state.

Recording, promoting, and changing the active workflow are separate decisions. A retrospective may surface a candidate lesson, but it must not silently rewrite the running contract. Adopt it through a deliberate change after reproduction and regression evidence at the narrowest canonical owner.

Do not promote a single anecdote as a universal invariant. Keep experimental thresholds and current model rankings in operational state until representative runs justify a stable rule.
